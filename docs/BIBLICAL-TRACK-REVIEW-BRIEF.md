# Reviewer Brief — Biblical Track

**For:** a practising Christian counsellor, pastor, or marriage ministry lead
**From:** Tether
**Subject:** Review of 90 daily reflection prompts for Christian couples

---

## What Tether is

Tether is a private app for two people in a relationship. Each partner answers
one short question a day — one sentence is enough — and neither sees the other's
answer until both have replied. The point is a small shared habit, not a
curriculum or a counselling programme.

Tether is **not** therapy, and it does not claim to be. It does not diagnose,
prescribe, or give clinical advice.

## Why the app has different tracks

Couples do not always share a worldview. Roughly four in ten marriages in the
United States cross religious lines. Most relationship apps assume a shared
faith, or strip faith out entirely. Tether lets **each partner choose their own
track** — Secular, Biblical, Vedic, or Quranic — so a Christian and a
non-Christian partner can each be spoken to in their own framework while sharing
the same daily practice.

The Biblical track is one of those four. It is written for Christian couples,
and for Christian partners in mixed-faith marriages.

## What we are asking you to do

Read the 90 prompts in **`ContentBiblical.swift`** (attached; also available as a
plain list on request) and tell us, in your own words:

1. **Is anything theologically wrong or misleading?**
   We are not asking whether you would have written it differently. We are
   asking whether any prompt would lead a couple to a wrong conclusion about
   God, Scripture, or marriage.

2. **Is anything pastorally harmful?**
   Could any prompt do damage in a struggling marriage — press a wound, invite
   disclosure that is not safe, or push a couple toward a conclusion they should
   reach with a pastor rather than an app?

3. **Does the arc hold?**
   The 90 days move through seven phases, and conflict work deliberately does
   not begin until day 43. Two weeks of gratitude and attention come first.
   Is that ordering sound, or does it delay something that should come earlier?

4. **Is the tone right?**
   We have aimed for warm and plain, not preachy. We paraphrase Scripture rather
   than quoting it at length, and we name no preferred translation. Is anything
   in the register off?

5. **What is missing?**
   What would a Christian couple need that we have not thought to ask?

You are free to mark individual prompts as **keep / rewrite / cut**, and to
rewrite them yourself. We would rather have 70 prompts that are right than 90
that are merely plausible.

---

## One decision we want you to scrutinise specifically

**We deliberately included no headship or submission language anywhere in the
conflict arc (days 43–56).**

Our reasoning: those passages are real, and they carry genuine weight within
many traditions. They are also, in our understanding, the single most commonly
weaponised text in coercive and abusive marriages. Tether ships an abuse
detector — if a user's words signal that they are being harmed, the app stops
normal output and shows crisis resources instead. We did not want the same
product to hand a controlling partner a verse, inside the very section that
teaches couples how to handle disagreement.

In place of submission, prompt 54 reads:

> *"What is one thing you could yield on in the next disagreement — not because
> you were wrong, but to show you are for them?"*

**We would genuinely like to be told we are wrong about this.** If you believe
this leaves the track theologically incomplete, say so, and tell us how you would
handle it. We would rather get this right than be safe.

Related: prompts 44 and 47 touch anger and words said in anger. We have tried to
keep them honest without making room for a user to justify harshness.

## Practicalities

- **Format:** a marked-up copy of the 90 prompts, or a written response, or a
  call — whatever is easiest for you.
- **Length:** no minimum. Five prompts you object to is more useful than a
  general endorsement.
- **Attribution:** with your permission we would like to credit you as the
  Biblical track's theological reviewer inside the app. Entirely your choice,
  and we will not list you without written consent.
- **Compensation:** we would like to pay you at your normal consulting or
  honorarium rate. Please tell us what that is.
- **Timeline:** we are not launching the Biblical track until this review is
  complete, so there is no artificial deadline. Take the time it needs.

## One more thing

If, after reading, your honest view is that an app should not be doing this at
all, we would like to hear that too. That is a real answer and we will take it
seriously.
