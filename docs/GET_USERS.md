# How Tether gets its first 100 couples

## First, the reframe

You called this do-or-die. It isn't, and here's the arithmetic:

> **$99 ÷ ~$34 net per annual subscriber ≈ 3 subscribers.**

**Three couples cover the entire Apple fee.** Everything after that is profit.
This is not a bet-the-house decision. It's three customers.

The real do-or-die is different, and it doesn't cost money:

> **Can you get 100 people to care?**

That takes work. It does not take $99. Which means you can *test it for free,
right now*, before you pay anything.

---

## The wedge: own one niche completely

Tether has one thing no competitor has:

> **Each partner chooses their own wisdom path.**

A Hindu–Christian couple, a Muslim–Jewish couple, a secular–religious couple.
**Each keeps their own tradition. The relationship is shared; the worldview is
personal.**

That is a real, specific, underserved audience — and it is *searchable*.

Generic couples apps compete with thousands. **Nobody serves interfaith
couples properly.** That's the whole distribution strategy: be the app for one
group, not a worse option for everyone.

---

## Where the first 100 actually are

**Communities (participate first, mention second):**
- Reddit: `r/interfaith`, `r/ABCDesis`, `r/MuslimMarriage`, `r/Jewish`,
  `r/Hinduism`, `r/relationship_advice`, `r/LongDistance`
- Facebook groups: search "interfaith couples", "interfaith marriage"
- Interfaith wedding planning communities
- Discord servers for mixed-faith relationships

**The rule:** contribute for two weeks before you mention the app once. Answer
questions. Be useful. Post your own story. Then — when someone asks "is there
anything for this?" — you have an answer.

Spam gets you banned and remembered badly. Contribution compounds.

**Content that works in this niche:**
- "How we handle Diwali *and* Christmas"
- "My parents still don't accept our marriage — what helped"
- "We raised our kids in both traditions. Here's what we did."
- Short video of a mixed-faith couple's daily ritual

This content performs well and costs nothing but honesty.

## The growth loop you already have

**A couples app is two-sided by design.** One user cannot use Tether alone —
they must invite their partner.

> **Every user brings a second user.**

That is a built-in 2× multiplier most apps would kill for. Two consequences:

1. **The invite flow must be effortless.** Currently it's local-only (CloudKit
   is the paid step). Before launch, make inviting a partner a two-tap
   experience.
2. **Growth is word-of-mouth by nature.** A couple that likes it tells other
   couples. That is the only marketing channel you can afford, and it happens
   to be the most effective one.

## App Store Optimization

Your listing is already written (`APP_STORE_LISTING.md`). What matters:

- **Title + subtitle carry the most weight.** "Tether: Couples Journal" /
  "One minute a day, together"
- **Keywords:** the 100 characters are the highest-leverage text you have.
  Target *interfaith*, *couples*, *marriage*, *daily*, *private* — not
  "relationship app", which is unwinnable.
- **Ratings move rankings more than anything else.** Ask every happy user. In
  person, at the right moment — not a popup.
- **Screenshots:** the first two decide the install. Lead with the reveal.

You already want to build in public for your iOS career. **This is the same
activity.** Post the journey — the landscape scenes, the encryption, the
interfaith design decision.

- LinkedIn, X, and a short devlog
- One honest post per week, not daily noise
- People follow *the making*, not the launch announcement

This gets you users *and* the job-readiness you're already chasing. Same work,
two outcomes.

## The 30-day plan

**Days 1–7 — Validate before paying**
- Post in 3 interfaith communities. Not about the app — about the problem.
- Ask: *"Would something like this help you?"*
- **If nobody responds, you've saved $99.** That's a win, not a failure.

**Days 8–14 — Ship**
- Buy the Apple account. TestFlight to 20 people.
- Recruit from the people who responded in week 1.
- Fix what breaks. It will break.

**Days 15–21 — The niche push**
- One piece of content per day for the interfaith angle
- Post your own couple's story if you have one
- Answer every single comment

**Days 22–30 — Reviews and loop**
- Ask every active user for a rating, personally
- Watch the invite flow — where do people drop off?
- Ship one improvement per week, publicly

## Honest expectations

| Milestone | Realistic |
|---|---|
| First 10 users | 1–2 weeks of real effort |
| First 100 users | 1–3 months |
| First paying subscriber | 50–200 active users |
| Covering the $99 | ~3 subscribers |
| Meaningful income | Probably never, and that's OK |

**Most indie apps get almost no users.** Not because they're bad — because
nobody told anyone. The difference is entirely in the doing.

---

## Kill criteria — decide these now

Write these down before you start, so you're not deciding emotionally later:

- **If 0 of 10 people you ask say "yes, I'd use that"** → stop. The idea
  needs rework, not marketing.
- **If after 3 months you have under 20 active users despite genuine effort**
  → the product isn't landing. Change something big or stop.
- **If people use it once and never return** → the ritual isn't compelling
  enough. Fix retention before chasing users.

Stopping on evidence is not failure. It's the cheapest thing you'll ever do.
