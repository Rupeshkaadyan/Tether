# The validation kit

Copy-paste ready. This is how you get certain — in about a week, for free.

**The rule: do not mention the app in the first message.** You are asking about
their life, not pitching. If you pitch first, you get polite lies.

---

## Message 1 — to one couple you know (DM or text)

Send this to 5–10 interfaith couples. Personal, not a broadcast.

> Hey — random question. You and [partner] come from different traditions,
> right? I've been thinking about how couples like you handle the religious
> side of things day to day — not the wedding, the ordinary Tuesday.
>
> Can I ask you two things? Genuinely just curious, not selling anything.

**Why it works:** it names something true about them that most people ignore.
Interfaith couples are used to being misunderstood. Being *seen* is rare enough
that people answer.

---

## Message 2 — the two questions

Only after they say yes.

> 1. Is there anything about being an interfaith couple that you wish someone
>    had made for you? Anything that's annoying or missing?
>
> 2. If there were a small daily thing you both did — one question a day, each
>    of you answers privately, then you see each other's — would that appeal to
>    you, or does it sound like homework?

**Listen for:**
- **"Oh my god yes, this thing"** → real pain. Build for it.
- **"That's nice"** → polite. Not pain. Keep asking.
- **"Sounds like homework"** → the ritual framing is wrong. Fix the framing.

**Do not defend the idea.** If they say it sounds like homework, write that
down and thank them. That's the most valuable sentence you'll hear all week.

---

## Reddit post — r/interfaith

Post this **only** after you've commented helpfully in the sub for a week or
two. Check each sub's self-promotion rules first.

**Title:** The thing nobody tells you about interfaith marriage is that it's
not the wedding — it's the Tuesday

**Body:**

> Six years in, and I've realised the hard parts were never the big ceremonies.
>
> It's the small stuff. Which festival we actually celebrate. What we tell the
> kids. How to say "I'm not religious in the way you are" without it landing as
> rejection.
>
> The thing I've found hardest is that most advice assumes one of you will
> convert, or that you'll both become "spiritual but not religious". Neither is
> true for us. We each kept our own thing. That's the whole point.
>
> Curious how others here handle it. Do you have a shared practice, or do you
> keep your traditions separate? And has anyone found anything that actually
> helps with the day-to-day, not just the theory?

**Why it works:** it's a real question, it names a real gap, and the comments
will tell you whether anyone wants what you're building — without you ever
mentioning it.

---

## The follow-up (only if people engage)

> This is going to sound like a plug, and it sort of is — I've been building
> something for exactly this. One question a day, each partner answers
> privately, then you see each other's. You each pick your own tradition, so
> nobody has to convert or compromise.
>
> It's not out yet. Would you want to try it when it is?

**Count the yeses.** That number is your answer.

---

## What the numbers mean

| Responses | What it means |
|---|---|
| **0 of 10 reply** | The problem isn't real, or you're asking the wrong people |
| **2–3 of 10 engage, 0 want it** | Real problem, wrong solution. Keep talking |
| **2–3 of 10 say "yes, tell me when"** | Enough to justify the $99 |
| **5+ of 10 say yes** | You have a product. Ship it |

**Two or three committed people is enough.** You don't need a hundred. You need
proof that *anyone* wants this badly enough to raise their hand before it
exists.

---

## The 7-day version

- **Day 1** — Send Message 1 to 5 couples you know
- **Day 2** — Send Message 2 to everyone who replied
- **Day 3** — Read r/interfaith for an hour. Comment helpfully on 3 posts
- **Day 4** — Comment on 3 more
- **Day 5** — Post the Reddit question
- **Day 6** — Answer every comment honestly
- **Day 7** — Count the people who said "tell me when"

**Then decide.**

---

## The honest part

If this week produces nothing, the $99 would have bought you an app nobody
wanted. That's the best $0 you'll ever spend.

If it produces two people who say *"please tell me when this exists"*, then
you're not gambling — **you already have customers waiting.**

Either way, you'll know. And you'll know it in a week instead of a year.
